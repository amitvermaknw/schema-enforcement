\label{sec:results:profiles}

Grouping models by dominant category gives four observed profiles
(\cref{fig:profiles}): \cat{markdown\_wrap} for Haiku~4.5 and Sonnet~5;
\cat{regex\_pattern} for the three GPT-5.6 models and gpt-4o-mini;
\cat{length\_bound} for Opus~4.8 and Sonnet~4.6; and
\cat{schema\_mirroring} for gpt-4o. These are profiles read off the
dominant category, not clusters from any statistical procedure. They are
not determined solely by vendor, model tier, or failure rate: gpt-4o and
gpt-4o-mini share a generation but not a profile, and gpt-4o-mini,
Sonnet~4.6 and gpt-4o fail at \pn{g.gpt-4o-mini@medium.rate},
\pn{g.claude-sonnet-4-6@medium.rate} and \pn{g.gpt-4o@medium.rate}, with
overlapping intervals, under three different dominant categories.
Dominant shares for low-rate models rest on few failures; Opus~4.8's
\pn{g.claude-opus-4-8@medium.dominant_share} share, for example, rests on
\pn{g.claude-opus-4-8@medium.failures} failures (95\% CI
\pn{g.claude-opus-4-8@medium.share.length_bound.ci}).

Some categories are confined to particular models in our data.
\cat{schema\_mirroring} accounts for \pn{g.gpt-4o@medium.dominant_share}
of gpt-4o failures and was not observed in any GPT-5.6 configuration we
tested. \cat{markdown\_wrap} was not observed for Sonnet~4.6 across
\pn{m.claude-sonnet-4-6.attempts} attempts or Opus~4.8 across
\pn{m.claude-opus-4-8.attempts}, yet it accounts for
\pn{g.claude-sonnet-5@medium.dominant_share} of Sonnet~5 failures and
\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.dominant_share} of Haiku~4.5 failures.
Haiku~4.5 and gpt-4o are each dominant in a category the other did not
produce in our observations. Among the
\pn{divergence.groups_qualifying} model--tier groups with at least 20
failures, the largest difference between two groups in the share of a
single category is \pn{divergence.top.0.pp} percentage points, attained
by \pn{divergence.top.n_tied} pairs, each setting Haiku~4.5 against a
group with no \cat{markdown\_wrap} failures (\cref{app:divergence}).

Profiles also vary with the task distribution. Sonnet~5 fails at nearly
the same rate on both domains (\pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@financebench.rate}
on FinanceBench, \pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@hotpotqa.rate} on HotpotQA),
but on HotpotQA \cat{markdown\_wrap} accounts for
\pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@hotpotqa.dominant_share} of its
\pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@hotpotqa.failures} failures, while on
FinanceBench no category clearly dominates its
\pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@financebench.failures}:
\cat{length\_bound} \pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@financebench.share.length_bound}
(95\% CI \pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@financebench.share.length_bound.ci}),
\cat{regex\_pattern} \pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@financebench.share.regex_pattern}
and \cat{markdown\_wrap} \pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@financebench.share.markdown_wrap}.
The observed profile is thus a property of model, constraints, prompt and
task distribution together. Opus~4.8 also changes dominant category
across domains, but its HotpotQA profile rests on
\pn{c.claude-opus-4-8@medium@hotpotqa.failures} failures and we do not
interpret it (\cref{app:breakdowns}).

A profile is not a property of the model alone. Sonnet~4.6 is
\cat{length\_bound}-dominant at the medium tier
(\pn{calib.medium.length_bound} of \pn{calib.medium.failures} failures).
With the caps raised in medium\_v2, \cat{length\_bound} falls to
\pn{calib.medium_v2.length_bound}, \cat{regex\_pattern} becomes dominant
(\pn{calib.medium_v2.regex_pattern} of \pn{calib.medium_v2.failures}), and
the failure rate moves from \pn{calib.medium.rate} to
\pn{calib.medium_v2.rate} (\cref{tab:calibration}).

The error locations identify which caps were binding:
\pn{calib.field.coverage_notes.n} \code{coverage\_notes} values of
\pn{calib.field.coverage_notes.min}--\pn{calib.field.coverage_notes.max}
characters against the 250 cap (mean overrun
\pn{calib.field.coverage_notes.mean_overrun}, maximum
\pn{calib.field.coverage_notes.max_overrun}), \pn{calib.field.answer.n}
\code{answer} values of
\pn{calib.field.answer.min}--\pn{calib.field.answer.max} against 400 (mean
overrun \pn{calib.field.answer.mean_overrun}), and
\pn{calib.field.fact.n} \code{fact} value of
\pn{calib.field.fact.max} characters against 400. These caps sat below
the lengths the model produced, not far below them. Because medium\_v2 is
a fresh generation, its result could in principle reflect changed output
rather than changed caps. We therefore revalidated all \pn{cf.n} stored
medium outputs, valid and failed, against the medium\_v2 schema. All
\pn{cf.invalid_to_valid.length_bound} \cat{length\_bound} failures pass,
no valid output becomes invalid, and the remaining
\pn{cf.samev2.failures} failures are the original \cat{regex\_pattern},
\cat{enum\_violation} and \cat{prose\_preamble} cases. Relaxing the caps
is sufficient to eliminate the observed length violations. This is an
offline revalidation, not a simulated medium\_v2 run: under the relaxed
schema the retries that followed those failures would not have occurred.

Loosening the identifier character class is the contrasting case. On
HotpotQA, gpt-5.6-sol and gpt-5.6-terra produced
\pn{pert.medium.identifiers} violating identifiers under the baseline
pattern and \pn{pert.medium_v3_loose_regex.identifiers} under the
loosened pattern. Leading-digit violations such as the example in
\cref{sec:intro} went from \pn{pert.medium.leading_digit} to
\pn{pert.medium_v3_loose_regex.leading_digit}, as the loosened class
permits them. Length overflows against the retained 31-character ceiling
occurred under both patterns (\pn{pert.medium.length_overflow} and
\pn{pert.medium_v3_loose_regex.length_overflow}); with a handful of
failures per cell we do not interpret that change or the cell rates
(\cref{app:perturbation}). The relaxation removed the sub-cause it
addressed and did not address length overflow, which the
\cat{regex\_pattern} label conceals because the ceiling is written into
the pattern's quantifier.

Because \cat{markdown\_wrap} takes precedence, it says nothing about what
lies inside the fence. We stripped the fence from every wrapped failure
and revalidated the payload (\cref{tab:fence}).
\pn{fence.reported.pure_share} validate; the rest raise a further error,
overwhelmingly on length: \pn{fence.reported.concealed.length_bound}
\cat{length\_bound}, \pn{fence.reported.concealed.regex_pattern}
\cat{regex\_pattern} and \pn{fence.reported.concealed.missing_field}
\cat{missing\_field}. The two wrapping models share a dominant category
but not what the wrapper hides: \pn{g.claude-sonnet-5@medium.fence_compound}
of Sonnet~5's \pn{g.claude-sonnet-5@medium.fence_n} wrapped failures are
compound (95\% CI \pn{g.claude-sonnet-5@medium.fence_compound_ci}),
against \pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.fence_compound_share} of
Haiku~4.5's \pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.fence_n} (95\% CI
\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.fence_compound_ci}). The contrast is not a
domain-mix artefact: on HotpotQA alone the compound shares are
\pn{c.claude-sonnet-5@medium@hotpotqa.fence_compound_share} and
\pn{c.claude-haiku-4-5@medium@hotpotqa.fence_compound_share}, and Haiku's
rises to \pn{c.claude-haiku-4-5@medium@financebench.fence_compound_share}
on FinanceBench. Counting co-occurrence, \cat{length\_bound} reaches
\pn{reported.length_bound_incl_concealed} instances against a primary
count of \pn{reported.cat.length_bound}, so single-primary-label counts
omit some detected co-occurring violations.

\paragraph{What the repair loop repairs.}
Concealment determines what retry feedback can fix. All
\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.fa.reached} of Haiku~4.5's first attempts
failed strict validation, every one of them fenced; we audited the
extraction (one first attempt per run-node, contiguous attempt numbers,
no attempts after a valid one, and fences confirmed on the raw text;
\cref{app:breakdowns}). On the second attempt, after the validation error
was returned, \pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.att1.valid} of
\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.att1.n} validated and fences persisted in
\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.att1.cat.markdown_wrap}; no later attempt
was fenced. What remained was length: \cat{length\_bound} accounts for
every failure from the third attempt onward, and all
\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.exhausted} run-nodes that exhausted their
retries ended on \cat{length\_bound}, \pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.exhausted.node.gather}
of them in gather. These are exactly the runs that did not complete. The
loop therefore did nearly all of the strict-conformance work for this
model (\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.fa.retry_success} of
\pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.fa.failed} failed first attempts eventually
validated), and its residual failures were the violations the fence had
concealed. Offline on the stored attempts, counting pure-formatting
failures as recovered leaves \pn{pooled.reported.residual_rate} of
attempts invalid (95\% CI \pn{pooled.reported.residual_ci}) against
\pn{pooled.reported.rate} strict, \pn{g.claude-haiku-4-5@medium.residual_rate}
for Haiku~4.5 and \pn{g.claude-sonnet-5@medium.residual_rate} for
Sonnet~5. These are not outcomes of rerunning the pipeline with fence
removal enabled, which would change retries and downstream exposure.

Concealment also changes how a prompt ablation reads. We ran three
system-prompt variants on Haiku~4.5 on HotpotQA: the baseline, an
aggressive format instruction, and a few-shot prompt
(\cref{tab:ablation}). The wrapping rate falls from
\pn{ablation.baseline.markdown_wrap} of attempts to
\pn{ablation.few_shot.markdown_wrap}, a relative reduction of
\pn{ablation.delta.markdown_wrap_rel}, and the failure rate from
\pn{ablation.baseline.failure_rate} to \pn{ablation.few_shot.failure_rate}.
The primary \cat{length\_bound} rate rises from
\pn{ablation.baseline.length_bound} to \pn{ablation.few_shot.length_bound},
which read alone suggests failures migrating from wrapping to length. But
a suppressed fence can expose a length violation that was already
present. Counting primary and concealed length violations together gives
\pn{ablation.baseline.length_bound_incl_concealed},
\pn{ablation.aggressive.length_bound_incl_concealed} and
\pn{ablation.few_shot.length_bound_incl_concealed}: the combined rate does
not rise as wrapping falls, so the rise in the primary rate requires no
increase in over-long output. We do not interpret the differences among
the combined rates, including the lower aggressive figure; the variant
cells are much smaller than the baseline cell, were run immediately
after the final baseline batch rather than interleaved with it, and are
not paired by question.
