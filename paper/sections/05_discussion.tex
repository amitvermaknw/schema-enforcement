\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Implications for pipeline authors.}
Three practices follow from the data, each at the scale of this corpus.
First, strip fences before validating, then revalidate and record what
remains: stripping alone recovers the pure-formatting share of wrapped
failures and would report the compound share as solved. Second, do not
express length bounds inside a regular expression. A \code{\{2,30\}}
quantifier turns an over-long identifier into a pattern violation, and
the natural remedy for a pattern violation, loosening the character
class, leaves it untouched; a separate \code{max\_length} would separate
the causes at validation time. Third, measure a field's observed length
distribution before fixing its cap. Sonnet~4.6's dominant mode came from
caps sitting modestly below its output lengths. Such a cap may still be a
real downstream requirement; the measurement tells an author which side
must move, not that the cap is wrong.

\paragraph{Durability.}
A reasonable objection is that providers are engineering these modes
away. Our cross-generation data speaks to both directions:
\cat{schema\_mirroring} dominates gpt-4o and was not observed in the
GPT-5.6 configurations we tested, while \cat{markdown\_wrap} was not
observed in Sonnet~4.6 and dominates Sonnet~5 and the current Haiku. What
changed across generations is which category dominates. A vocabulary for
the categories remains useful as the distribution moves, which is a
weaker claim than predicting that any particular mode persists.

\paragraph{Scope and generality.}
The schemas are ours, and one ablation showed that a cap we set was
binding below the output lengths of one model; a different author would
produce a different distribution over categories. We cover two vendors by
design, trading breadth for several generations and capability tiers per
vendor, which the within-family comparisons above require. A third vendor
may exhibit modes our taxonomy lacks. Questions are fixed dataset
prefixes, and the design is not fully crossed. We evaluate conformance
only, not whether extracted content is correct.

\paragraph{Measurement.}
Attempts within a run are not independent, and repair attempts, which are
conditioned on a prior failure, make up a model-dependent share of each
rate, so intervals over attempts are indicative. Five models reject an
explicit temperature, the Anthropic and OpenAI branches use different
token ceilings, and some questions were run more than once
(\cref{app:manifest} shows this does not materially change the most
affected cell). Classifier agreement is development-set agreement from a
single labeller who wrote the classifier. Primary categories are
single-valued, and we quantify co-occurrence only beneath fences.

\paragraph{What is not tested.}
Among attempts whose model-level validators ran, no cross-field
violation occurred (\cref{app:crossfield}), but attempts rejected at an
earlier stage, including during JSON parsing, never reached those checks,
so the zero describes a filtered subset. Cross-node referential
integrity---whether a later node's identifiers appear in an earlier
node's entities---is not implemented in our schemas. It is expressible in
Pydantic by passing the earlier output as validation context
(\code{model\_validate\_json(data, context=\ldots)}), and is the natural
next measurement.
