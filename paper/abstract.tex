\begin{abstract}
Prompt-and-validate pipelines---an ordinary chat endpoint, a Pydantic
model and a repair loop---are a common way to obtain structured output
from hosted language models, and their evaluation usually reduces to a
validity rate. We define a taxonomy of schema-conformance failures for
this paradigm, assigned by a deterministic classifier over the raw
completion and the validator's error, and apply it to
\pn{reported.attempts} generation attempts across \pn{reported.cells}
configurations spanning \pn{reported.models} models from two vendors,
three schema tiers and two task domains. Three findings follow that a
rate cannot express. First, models with similar failure rates fail in
different ways, and a model's dominant category is a joint property of
the model and the constraint surface: relaxing length caps moves one
model from a length-dominant to a pattern-dominant profile, and
revalidating its stored completions shows the change is mechanical.
Second, the most frequent category, markdown wrapping, conceals a
further violation in \pn{fence.reported.compound_share} of cases, ranging
from \pn{fence.claude-sonnet-5.compound_share} for one wrapping model to
\pn{fence.claude-haiku-4-5.compound_share} for another, so
fence-stripping alone is not a repair. Third, because wrapping takes
precedence in classification, prompt variants that suppress fences
raise the primary length-violation share without raising the combined
rate of primary and concealed length violations; primary labels alone
would suggest a migration between failure modes. We release the
classifier, raw completions and annotation record.
\end{abstract}
